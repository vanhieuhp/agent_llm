I strongly support the motion: **there should be strict laws to regulate LLMs**.

LLMs are not ordinary software. They can generate persuasive misinformation, enable mass fraud, spread bias at scale, and be used to automate harmful activity faster than society can react. Without strict legal controls, the risks are not hypothetical—they are already visible in deepfake scams, impersonation, propaganda, and the leakage of sensitive data. When a technology can influence millions instantly, “self-regulation” is not enough.

Strict laws are necessary for three main reasons:

1. **Public safety**
   LLMs can be used to produce convincing phishing messages, fake identities, harmful instructions, and disinformation. The damage can affect individuals, businesses, and democratic institutions. Laws should require clear safeguards, testing, and accountability before deployment.

2. **Accountability**
   Right now, when an LLM causes harm, responsibility is often blurred between developers, deployers, and users. Strict regulation would force clear legal liability. If companies profit from powerful models, they must also bear responsibility for foreseeable harm.

3. **Protection of rights**
   LLMs are trained on massive amounts of data and can reproduce bias, invade privacy, and reinforce discrimination. Strict laws should require transparency, data protections, auditing, and independent oversight so these systems do not violate people’s rights.

The core principle is simple: **the more powerful the technology, the stronger the rules must be**. We regulate medicines, cars, banks, and planes because their misuse can seriously harm society. LLMs now have comparable scale and influence over information, behavior, and decision-making. Leaving them lightly governed would be reckless.

Strict regulation does not mean banning innovation. It means ensuring innovation is safe, accountable, and human-centered. Without strong laws, the public pays the price while companies collect the profit. For that reason, this motion is not only reasonable—it is necessary.
